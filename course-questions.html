Getting started

Where do I start?

If you know the week, start its note sheet. I do not know what to do. What is the exact order?

Start with the pre-work, in this exact order. One: read the note sheet questions first, before anything else. Two: preview the text with those questions in your head. Three: organize your notes so you know where to find things. Do not make flashcards; organize. Four: practice answering the questions the best you can, from what you already know, in your first color, on the master note sheet you will draw and create from. Five: now watch the video for that concept; the concept name and number are at the top of the prompt. Six: as you watch, add what you did not know in a second color, from the video or any other digging you do. Your first color is your baseline, what you already had, especially if you wrote it from memory. The second color is exactly what you need to commit to memory. Then turn it in. For lab: run PhysioEx first, then complete the worksheet, then the clinical correlations. And if a step genuinely does not work for the way you learn, see the next question; the method bends, the course requirements do not.

What if that order does not work for me?

Then adjust it; the order is the default because it works for most people, not a rule you can fail. Adapt it to how you learn: if reading first truly stalls you, watch the concept video first, then close the loop by answering the prompt from memory afterward, in your first color, and add what you still had to look up in the second. If writing is slow for you, talk your answer out loud first, then write it. What has to survive any adaptation, because it is how this course is built: at some point you answer from memory rather than while looking, your two colors stay honest about what was baseline and what was new, PhysioEx runs before the worksheet, and the Sunday deadline does not move. Inside those walls, build the version that fits you, and if you want help designing it, bring it to office hours; that is a great use of them.

Grading

How is my grade calculated?

Five components: competency note sheets 20 percent, three midterms 40 percent, lab 15 percent, book problems 15 percent, discussions 10 percent. Full detail in syllabus section 07.

Is there a curve?

No. The scale is fixed: A is 89.5 to 100, B is 79.5 to 89.4, C is 69.5 to 79.4, D is 59.5 to 69.4.

Is there extra credit?

No individual extra credit, and asking will not create any. If I ever offer extra credit, I offer it to the whole class at once.

What grade do I need for nursing programs?

Most health programs require a B or better in this course, and many effectively require an A. Check your specific program's requirements with a counselor: Yuba counseling.

Can I take this Pass or No Pass?

No. This course is letter grade only.

How are note sheets graded?

You submit the whole sheet. I pick five prompts and grade those for accuracy, and you will not know which five ahead of time, so every prompt has to be real. The rest is checked for completion.

How are discussions graded?

One post a week that does two jobs: works through something from that week's physiology, and says something honest about your own thinking on it. Initial post Friday 10:00 pm, peer replies Sunday 10:00 pm.

How is the lab graded?

Both parts, always: PhysioEx must show complete in Pearson, and you turn in the worksheet and data record, plus the clinical correlation questions in weeks that have them. One without the other earns nothing.

How are book problems graded?

Assigned Silverthorn problems each week, handwritten, submitted in Canvas by Sunday 10:00 pm. Problem numbers are on each week's page.

How much is each midterm worth?

The three midterms together are 40 percent, so each is roughly 13 percent of your final grade.

What is the biggest part of my grade?

The midterms at 40 percent. But the weekly work, 60 percent combined, is what makes the midterms passable, and it is also the practice for them.

Can my grade be rounded up?

The scale already has the rounding built in: 89.5 is an A. Beyond that, no, because an exception for one student is an unfairness to everybody else.

Do you drop the lowest anything?

No drops. Every week counts, which is also why no single week can sink you.

What happens if I bomb one midterm?

One midterm is about 13 percent of the grade. Come to office hours, find the gap with the Mastery OS, and fix it before the next block, because physiology compounds.

Where can I see exactly what is graded?

The component table in syllabus section 07, and the Competency Packet for exactly what content is fair game. Nothing outside the packet is graded.

Why are note sheets worth so much?

Twenty percent because they are the pre work that makes everything else possible, and they double as your midterm study guide, so the points pay you twice.

Do typed assignments count?

No. Unless an assignment says otherwise, everything you turn in is handwritten and hand drawn. Typed work earns a zero. See syllabus section 11.

Why handwritten?

Drawing a pathway by hand is how your brain builds it, and it is how the midterms work, so it is how the practice works.

How fast are things graded?

Weekly work is typically graded within the week after it comes in. Midterm videos take longer; watch Canvas announcements.

Can I resubmit something to raise the grade?

No. Graded work stays graded. The next week is always the better investment.

What does mastery based mean here?

Attending and turning things in does not by itself earn the grade. The study hours and what you can actually do are what do.

Where does participation show up in the grade?

Participation in an online course is submitted work. The weekly note sheet is the heartbeat; if it is coming in, you are participating.

Are the weights ever adjusted during the term?

No. The five components and their weights are fixed for the term: 20, 40, 15, 15, 10. What you see in section 07 is the whole deal.

Where did Scholar Points or bonus systems go?

This course does not use them. Five components, no curve, no extra credit. Simple on purpose.

Note sheets

What is a note sheet?

A weekly sheet with one prompt for each competency you must be able to do that week. You complete it from the reading and the videos before anything else. It is the first step of every week.

When is the note sheet due?

Sunday at 10:00 pm, with everything else.

Which five prompts get graded?

You will not know ahead of time. That is the point: every prompt has to be real.

Can I type my note sheet?

No. Handwritten and hand drawn, then photographed or scanned into a PDF for Canvas.

What does a good note sheet answer look like?

Two colors telling the truth. First color: your best answer from memory and the text preview. Second color: what the video and your digging added. A drawing or worked explanation you could teach from, not a copied sentence. If your sheet can carry you through the practice round without the book, it is good.

Where do I find each week's note sheet?

On the week's page, step 1, posted when the week opens.

Do note sheets help with the midterms?

They are the midterm study guide. Every midterm pathway starts life as a note sheet prompt.

What if I cannot finish every prompt?

Turn in what you have by Sunday 10:00 pm. A partially complete sheet earns partial completion credit; a late sheet costs half; after 24 hours it is a zero.

Can I work on note sheets with classmates?

Study together, absolutely. But what you turn in must be your own work in your own hand from your own head.

How long should a note sheet take?

Plan on several focused hours across the week. It is most of your prepare and watch time, which is why it comes first.

What is the two color rule?

Your first color is your baseline: what you can answer from memory and the text preview, before the video. The second color is everything you add from the video or your own digging afterward. The result reads at a glance: color one is what you already had, color two is exactly what you still need to commit to memory. Your studying aims at the second color.

Do I need special supplies?

Paper, pens in two colors, and a way to photograph or scan to PDF. That is it.

My handwriting is bad. Will I lose points?

No. Legible is the bar, beautiful is not. If I can read it and follow the drawing, it grades fine.

Do I submit the note sheet even on a midterm window week?

Yes. Weeks 6 and 11 run their own full week while the window is open. The note sheet is due Sunday 10:00 pm as always.

What if I lose my note sheet before submitting?

This is why you photograph work the moment you finish it. Rebuild what you can before 10:00 pm and submit; partial beats zero, and the late policy has no lost paper clause.

Discussions

How do discussions work?

One post a week, not two. It works through something from that week's physiology and says something honest about your own thinking on that same material, one post that carries both.

When are discussion posts due?

Initial post Friday at 10:00 pm, so there is something to reply to. Peer replies Sunday at 10:00 pm.

What does the honest thinking part look like?

Where did your prediction turn out to be wrong, and what were you assuming that made it seem right? What did you have to go back and relearn? Which part still feels shaky when you explain it without looking?

How many replies do I owe?

Peer replies are due Sunday at 10:00 pm; the assignment in Canvas states the count. Replies that engage the physiology count; replies that say great post do not.

Can I post my discussion late?

The late policy applies: up to 24 hours late is an automatic 50 percent, after that a zero. And a late initial post also shortchanges your classmates who needed something to reply to.

What should I write about?

Anything real from that week: a loop that surprised you, a prediction that failed, a mechanism that finally clicked. Specific beats general every time.

Is there a length requirement?

The Canvas assignment states any minimum. The real bar is substance: physiology plus your thinking, in your own words.

Can I use AI to write my discussion post?

No. Discussions are graded work, and graded work is your own words from your own head. Use AI to study; write the post yourself.

Why is Friday the deadline for the first post?

So the weekend conversation exists. If everyone posts Sunday at 9:58 pm, nobody replies to anyone.

Do discussions cover exam material?

They are built from the same week's competencies, so yes, working them is studying.

What is Week 1's discussion?

Your digital vision board: one image showing who you are, what matters to you, why you are here, and where you are going, posted with a short video of you walking us through it. Post by Friday, September 11, replies by Sunday, September 13, 10:00 pm.

Where are the discussions?

In Canvas, in that week's module. The week page's Discuss step reminds you every week.

Do replies have deadlines different from the post?

Yes: initial post Friday 10:00 pm, peer replies Sunday 10:00 pm, every week, no variation.

Can I see classmates' posts before I write mine?

Canvas may hide posts until you post, and either way write yours from your own week's work. The honesty half cannot be borrowed.

Midterms

How do the midterms work?

You draw a physiological pathway and teach it on video, out loud, no notes: draw the mechanism, label it, explain each step and why it happens. Not multiple choice.

When is Midterm 1?

The window runs Monday, October 12 at 8:00 am to Sunday, October 18 at 10:00 pm. It covers Weeks 1 to 5.

When is Midterm 2?

Monday, November 16 at 8:00 am to Sunday, November 22 at 10:00 pm. It covers Weeks 6 to 10, and it closes the day after the November 21 withdrawal deadline on purpose, so you see your exam before that decision is forever.

When is Midterm 3?

Monday, December 14 at 8:00 am to Wednesday, December 16 at 10:00 pm, with the term. It covers Weeks 11 to 15.

Is the midterm at a set time?

No. Each midterm is a window, not an hour. It opens Monday at 8:00 am and closes at 10:00 pm on its closing day. You pick when inside the window.

Can I use notes on the midterm?

No notes, no book, no screen beyond the recording. You, a pen, paper, and your own head. That is the point of the format.

How do I record my video?

A phone propped up works fine. Show your paper, draw as you talk, and speak like you are teaching a classmate. Submission instructions are on the exam in Canvas.

How long should my video be?

Long enough to draw and teach the pathway completely. The exam instructions give the range. Rambling does not earn points; complete teaching does.

What if I mess up mid recording?

Pause, correct yourself, and keep teaching, exactly like a real teacher would. A corrected mistake shows understanding. Re record if you must, inside the window.

What gets asked on a midterm?

Pathways drawn from that block's competencies in the packet. If you can teach every pathway your note sheets built, you are ready.

How do I know the format before it counts?

Every single week includes a five minute ungraded practice round in exactly the midterm format. By Midterm 1 the format is familiar.

Are the midterms cumulative?

Each covers its own block of weeks. But physiology compounds, so Midterm 3's pathways stand on everything before them.

What if my internet dies during the window?

The window is a full week precisely so a bad day cannot sink you. Record early; submit early. A video submitted Tuesday cannot be lost on Sunday.

Can I retake a midterm?

No retakes and no reopening the window for one student that everyone else met. If something serious is happening in your life, talk to me about your options in the course as a whole.

How should I study for a midterm?

Draw pathways from memory, then teach them out loud with no notes, then check the drawing against your note sheet. The Mastery OS gap finder tells you which pathways to hit first.

Do the midterms test lab skills?

Yes, lab reasoning shows up in the teach backs: reading a trace, predicting what a change does, explaining a value.

Is there a final exam?

Midterm 3 is your last assessment. There is no separate final during a finals week; the term simply ends December 16.

What is a draw and teach exam like the first time?

Strange for about three minutes, then normal. That is why the weekly practice rounds exist. Students consistently report the format shows what they know better than any bubble sheet did.

Can someone else be in the room when I record?

You must be alone with no help of any kind during the exam. The video is you teaching, solo.

What if I freeze on camera?

Breathe, look at your drawing, and start with what the pathway accomplishes. The practice rounds exist so that by exam week your hands know the drill even when your nerves do not.

When do midterm grades come back?

Videos take longer to grade than sheets. Watch Canvas announcements for the timeline after each window closes.

What happens during exam weeks to regular work?

The weeks keep running. Weeks 6 and 11 carry their own work while a window is open, so plan the exam around the week, not instead of it.

Can I choose which pathway I teach?

The exam tells you what to draw and teach. The weekly practice rounds sample the same competencies, so nothing on the exam is a stranger.

What supplies do I need for the exam video?

Blank paper, two pen colors, decent light, and a propped phone. Set them out before you press record.

Weekly virtual labs in PhysioEx through Pearson. You generate and interpret real physiological output: traces, curves, values, calculations. The worksheet you complete from it is the graded work.

What exactly earns the lab credit?

Both parts, in this order: run PhysioEx first and get it showing complete in Pearson, then complete the worksheet and data record, then the clinical correlation questions in weeks that have them. One without the other earns nothing.

I did the worksheet but PhysioEx shows incomplete. Do I get credit?

Not until PhysioEx shows complete. The worksheet is only graded once Pearson shows the lab done.

How long does a lab take?

Plan two to three hours: run the simulation, record your data, work the questions, and the clinical correlations where they exist.

Can I do the lab before the week opens?

Deadlines and late work

When is everything due?

Sunday at 10:00 pm, every week, all fifteen weeks. Discussions run ahead: initial post Friday 10:00 pm. Week 15 closes Wednesday, December 16 at 10:00 pm with the term.

What is the late policy?

Up to 24 hours late: automatic 50 percent. After 24 hours: zero, no make up. No exceptions, not for family, travel, work, other obligations, or emergencies. See syllabus section 12.

Why are there no exceptions, even for emergencies?

Because the cushion is built in before the deadline, not after it. The material posts weeks ahead and nothing meets at a set time, so the week's work never has to be waiting on the day life goes sideways.

What if Canvas goes down Sunday night?

Submit early and it cannot happen to you. If a verified outage hits the whole class at a deadline, I will address it for the whole class in an announcement.

Do time zones matter?

Deadlines are 10:00 pm Pacific, the college's time. If you travel, submit on Pacific time.

Can I get an extension if I ask before the deadline?

No. What I can do before a deadline is help you triage: message me and we will decide what to finish first. What I cannot do is reopen a deadline for one student that everyone else met.

What counts as submitted?

Uploaded in Canvas, on the right assignment, before 10:00 pm. Check for the confirmation screen. A file sitting on your desktop is not submitted.

What if I upload the wrong file?

Fix it before the deadline; Canvas keeps the newest submission. Discovering it after the deadline falls under the late policy, so open your submission and check it.

Is there a grace period?

The 24 hour half credit window is the grace period, and it costs half. Treat 10:00 pm as real.

When does a week open?

Monday morning, except Week 1 which opens Tuesday, September 8. Everything for the week posts by its Monday.

What is due during Thanksgiving week?

Week 12 runs normally and the Sunday deadline does not move. Plan ahead; the material posts early precisely for weeks like that one.

What is the single best deadline habit?

Submit by Thursday what you can, and never let Sunday be the first time you open the week. The students who plan to find time Sunday usually find something else instead.

Does the late policy apply to midterms?

Midterms have their own windows, a full week each. Inside the window, any time. After it closes, it is closed.

Time and workload

How many hours a week does this course take?

The official floor is about 14. Realistically plan 16 to 20 to do well, and 20, sometimes closer to 30, if you need the A that health programs require. The honest numbers are in syllabus section 08.

Why so many hours for one class?

It is a 4 unit lab science: 216 learning hours by the official course outline, compressed into 15 weeks. Online does not make it smaller; it only means nobody schedules the hours for you.

I work full time. Can I pass this course?

Students with full time jobs pass this course every term. What they share: fixed work times chosen at the start of the week and protected like shifts.

What if I am taking anatomy at the same time?

Budget extra hours: you will be teaching yourself the parts on your own time while learning how they work in mine. Use the anatomy refresher to close gaps early.

How should I split my hours across the week?

A workable shape: note sheet and videos across two or three sittings early in the week, discussion by Friday, lab and problems by Saturday, review and the practice round Sunday. Never all of it Sunday.

How do I plan a week when I know a busy stretch is coming?

Work ahead the week before. The material posts weeks ahead precisely so a trip or a work crunch never has to collide with a deadline.

What is the busiest week of the term?

Exam window weeks, 6 and 11, because the window overlaps a normal week. Front load those weeks and record your exam early in the window.

Is Week 15 really only three days?

Yes: Monday, December 14 through Wednesday, December 16. It is deliberately light, and Midterm 3's window is those same days.

What should I cut when I fall behind?

Nothing silently. Message me and we triage together. Usually: current week's note sheet first, then lab, then catch up. Never skip the current week to redo a graded past one.

How many hours before each midterm?

If you kept the weekly rhythm, the window week needs review, not relearning: several sessions of drawing and teaching pathways from memory. Cramming a block in a weekend does not survive a teach back.

Can I finish the whole course early?

No, the weeks open on schedule, and the rhythm is the pedagogy. You can read ahead as far as you like.

How should I plan my study week?

Treat the plan as a hypothesis. Sunday: write the week's plan with real hours in real slots. All week: work it and notice where it breaks. Next Sunday: revise based on what actually happened, not what you meant to happen. Students who plan, monitor, and revise outperform students who simply work harder; effort without monitoring is one of the most reliable predictors of a bad outcome.

Studying

What is the best way to study for this course?

Retrieve, do not reread. Draw pathways from memory, teach them out loud, run your recall cards, and keep an error log of every wrong answer. Rereading feels productive and is not; the research on this is decades deep and very consistent.

How do I use the Mastery OS?

Open the OS, let it track your competencies, run the daily recall cards, and clear your weakness dashboard every Sunday night. Little and often beats big and rare.

What are recall cards?

Short retrieval questions built from the competencies. Answer before you flip. Getting it wrong then correcting is the studying; smooth rereading is not.

What is the gap finder?

The OS view that shows which competencies are weak right now, so your next hour goes where it earns the most.

How often should I run cards?

Daily beats weekly at the same total minutes. Ten focused minutes a day keeps three hundred cards warm.

Should I make my own flashcards too?

No, and this is deliberate: do not make flashcards from your notes, organize the notes instead, so you always know where to find things. The OS recall cards already cover the 268 competencies; your scarce hours are better spent answering prompts, drawing, and teaching.

How do I memorize pathways?

You do not memorize them, you rebuild them. Draw from a blank page, check against your note sheet, fix, and draw again tomorrow. Two or three rebuilds and it is yours.

What does teach it out loud actually do?

Speaking forces sequence and causality. The gaps you can hide from your eyes you cannot hide from your own ears. It is also literally the exam format.

Is rereading the textbook studying?

It is warming up. Rereading and rewatching are passive, and passive time feels like progress while producing very little of it. The Silverthorn reading feeds the note sheet; retrieval, drawing from memory, and explaining out loud are the studying.

How do I study with the videos?

Answer the prompt first, in your first color, from what you already know. Then watch that concept's video, name and number at the top of the prompt, and add what you did not know in your second color. The video lands differently when it is answering questions you already tried; watching first wastes the attempt.

What should Sunday review look like?

Clear the weakness dashboard, retry this week's error log rows cold, do the five minute practice round, and skim next week's page so Monday starts warm.

Can I study with other people?

Yes, and teaching each other pathways is the best group activity in this course. Just remember the turned in work is yours alone.

What do I do with a concept that will not stick?

Draw it, teach it to the wall, ask Hootie or the discussion board, bring it to Wednesday office hours, and put its cards on daily rotation. Stubborn concepts fall to repetition plus a second explanation.

Is AI allowed for studying?

Encouraged. Have it quiz you, explain a concept a second way, or listen to you teach and ask follow ups. For graded work, your own words in your own hand, always.

How is studying physiology different from anatomy?

Anatomy asks what and where; physiology asks how and what happens if. Study by predicting and explaining, not listing. If you can answer what happens if this goes up, you are studying correctly.

What is the practice round for?

Five ungraded minutes each week in exactly the midterm format, so the format costs you nothing by the time it counts. Do it every week; it is the cheapest insurance in the course.

How do I know if I am actually ready for a midterm?

Draw and teach each pathway in the block from a blank page with no notes. Where you can, you are ready. Where you cannot, that is the list.

What is an error log and should I keep one?

Yes, keep one. Every wrong answer is a question you do not yet know how to ask. Log one row per miss: what I thought, what is correct, why I was wrong, what I will remember. A week later, try the same question again without looking. Retire a row only when you can get its whole type right cold, not the first time you get it right by luck.

How do I learn from a graded exam?

Sit with the graded exam and classify every lost point by error type: did not study it, studied but did not understand it, understood but could not apply it, was missing a foundation from an earlier course, or misread the question. Each type has a different fix, and until you know which type you are losing points to, you are guessing at what to change. Fifteen honest minutes per exam.

Does spacing really beat cramming?

At the same total hours, yes, and it is not close. One day of cramming and seven days of spaced work can contain identical hours and score very differently, because memory is built by returning to material after partly forgetting it. This is why the recall cards run daily and why the weekly rhythm exists.

How do I know if my studying is active or passive?

Rate an hour honestly on a five point scale: rereading and watching are passive, retrieval, self testing, and explaining out loud are active. If most of your hours sit at the passive end, that, more than total hours, is usually the problem. Move one hour a day from rereading to drawing from memory and watch what happens.

Why does effective studying feel strange at first?

Because it conflicts with how you have always studied. Testing yourself before you feel ready, drawing from a blank page, logging your errors, all of it feels harder and less smooth than rereading. The strangeness is not a sign the method is wrong. It is a sign that what you have always done is not what the research recommends. Give it two honest weeks.

Succeeding online, anywhere

What makes online classes harder than in person?

Nothing about the content. What is missing is the schedule someone else built: the room, the seat time, the classmates who notice your absence. Online students who succeed rebuild those on purpose.

What is the single most important online habit?

A written weekly schedule with your study blocks placed like shifts, made Sunday for the week ahead, and protected like a job.

How do I make myself start when nobody is watching?

Shrink the start: open the page, set a timer for ten minutes, begin the first prompt. Starting is the hard part; momentum does the rest. Never wait to feel ready.

Where should I study?

Same place, same times, phone out of reach. Your brain learns that the chair means work. A kitchen table at a fixed hour beats a perfect desk used at random.

How do I stop procrastinating in an online class?

Deadlines you set beat deadlines you dread: give every task your own due date two days before the real one. And do the dreaded task first each session while your will is fresh.

How often should I check in on an online course?

Log in at least three times a week even when nothing is due. Online courses punish the out of sight, out of mind reflex more than anything else.

How do I take an online class seriously while working?

Tell the people in your life your study blocks like you would tell them shifts. The class is real; treat its hours as real and others will too.

What do successful online students do that others skip?

They read the syllabus once completely, they submit early, they email professors like professionals, and they ask questions the same week the confusion starts.

How do I email a professor properly?

Course and section in the subject, your full name, one clear question, and what you already tried. Professors move fastest for students who make the question easy to answer.

What is netiquette and does it matter?

Write online the way you would speak in a seminar: respectful, on topic, no all caps, disagree with ideas rather than people. It matters because your classmates are real and your words are permanent.

How do I stay motivated for fifteen weeks?

Attach the course to the reason you enrolled, and keep the reason visible: the program you are entering, the patient you will someday treat. Then trust the routine on the days motivation does not show up, because routine outlasts mood.

How do I avoid falling behind without noticing?

A written self check, two minutes every Sunday: did everything go in, what is my current standing, what is next week, and what will I change. Students chronically overestimate their own understanding, and the gap is largest exactly where the stakes are highest. Casual reflection, I think I know this, does not close that gap. Written and specific does.

What do I do the day life blows up my plan?

Salvage the highest value hour: submit whatever is closest to done, message the professor factually and briefly, and rebuild next week's plan the same day. One bad day becomes a bad month only through silence.

Are online classes easier?

No, and believing they are is the most expensive myth in higher education. Same units, same hours, minus the built in structure. Plan accordingly and they are absolutely passable.

How do I build classmates online?

Use the discussions like a room: reply to the same few people, answer questions you know, form a two person message thread with someone whose posts you respect. Two allies change a term.

What tech habits prevent disasters?

Save in the cloud, photograph physical work immediately, keep a backup way online in mind such as a phone hotspot or the library, and never schedule your first upload for the deadline hour.

How do I read a syllabus efficiently?

Once fully at the start, then use it as a reference: deadlines, grading, late policy, contact rules. Most course questions students ask are answered in the syllabus they skimmed.

How do I handle a class where I never see anyone?

Show up in the ways that exist: real discussion posts, office hours even once, questions asked publicly. Presence online is built from small deliberate acts.

When should I contact the professor versus figure it out myself?

Try the syllabus and the course pages for ten minutes first. Then ask. The wrong amounts are zero and instantly; the right amount is after one honest attempt.

What does submit early actually buy me?

Everything: technology cannot ambush you, the late policy cannot touch you, and Sunday evening belongs to review instead of panic. Early is the cheapest advantage online.

What should I do in the last week of any online course?

Confirm every submission went in, check the gradebook line by line, and email about discrepancies immediately, not after grades post. Then close the term knowing the record matches the work.

How do I come back after disappearing for a week?

Do not wait to feel caught up. Submit the current week first, message the professor briefly and factually, then decide what past work still changes anything. Forward beats backward.

What is the best question to ask in any online course?

Here is what I understood, here is where it breaks, what am I missing. It shows your work and gets a real answer fast.
