      Why is high CO2 acidic?

      CO2 combines with water to make carbonic acid, which releases H+:

      CO2 + H2O ⇌ H2CO3 ⇌ H+ + HCO3−

      More CO2 means more H+, so pH falls. That is why PaCO2 runs opposite to pH: high PaCO2 is acidic and low PaCO2 is alkaline. HCO3− is a base, so it runs the same direction as pH.

      Key terms

      Acidemia and alkalemia
      The state of the blood itself: pH below 7.35 is acidemia, pH above 7.45 is alkalemia.
      Acidosis and alkalosis
      The process pushing pH in that direction. You can have an acidosis even when compensation has brought the pH back into the normal range.
      Primary disorder
      The problem that started the change. Its value moves pH in the same direction that pH actually went.
      Compensation
      The other system's response. The lungs adjust PaCO2 within minutes to hours; the kidneys adjust HCO3− over several days. Compensation pushes pH back toward normal, so the compensating value moves opposite to the primary one.
      Base excess (BE)
      How much acid or base it would take to bring the blood back to pH 7.40 if PaCO2 were a normal 40 mmHg, at body temperature, 37 °C (98.6 °F). Because it removes the effect of CO2, base excess shows only the metabolic (kidney) side. A negative value is called a base deficit and means a metabolic acid load. A positive value means extra base.
      Mixed disorder
      Two primary problems at once. In the mixed disorders in this trainer, the lungs and the metabolic side both push pH the same way, so neither one is compensating.
      Hypoxemia
      Low oxygen in arterial blood, shown by a PaO2 below 80 mmHg.
