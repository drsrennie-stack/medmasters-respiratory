How to use this page

Every competency below gets one box on your paper sheet. Two prompts are offered for each. Pick whichever one you want and do that one. If you want to do both, nothing is stopping you, but you are only graded on one per competency.

The tick boxes are for you. They live in your browser, they do not report to Canvas, and I never see them. They exist so you can put this down on Wednesday and know where you were.

Your sheet, two ways

Same boxes, same labels, same grading either way. Pick the one that fits how you work.

Print the built sheet

The sheet is already built for this week, one box per competency, and it prints clean: boxes only, no directions, because the directions live on this page.

Open and print the sheet

Printed and hand drawn only. Work on the paper, in your two colors. Typing on it, or drawing on the PDF with a stylus, earns a zero.

Rule your own instead

No printer needed, and nothing lost. Rule the boxes onto your own paper, one per competency, everything hand drawn.

The boxes and labels it must have

The labeling is not optional: competency number and name, prompt letter, your name and date, and the box number, on every box.

The two colors

First pass, color one. Book and your own notes, before the videos. Sparse. Mini mind maps, lists, sequences, flow charts. Not paragraphs. Leave real space in the box, because you are coming back to it.

Second pass, color two. After you watch the videos, add to the same box. Corrections, the parts you had wrong, the connections you missed. Do not start a new box and do not erase the first pass. The gap between the two colors is the whole point, and it is what I look at first.

What a box looks like on paper

What makes this one easy to grade. The header names the competency and the prompt. The first pass is sparse and left space. The second pass went into that space rather than onto a new page. The key says which color is which. Nothing on it is a paragraph.

Label every box the same way

Across the top of each box, in this order:

  • Competency number and name, copied from this page. The number matters more than the name, because it is how I find it.
  • Which prompt you chose, A or B.
  • Your name and the date you did the first pass.
  • Box number out of 14, so a missing one is obvious to both of us.

Put the color key once, on the front page. You do not need it on all fourteen.

How it is graded

Fourteen boxes. I grade five of them for accuracy, chosen at random and not announced, and check the rest for completion. That means the way to do well is to do all fourteen properly rather than guessing which five I will land on.

Rubric, each criterion out of 5
Criterion5, full marks3, partial1, not yet
Labeling Every box carries competency number and name, prompt letter, name, date and box number. Color key present. Drawings have their parts labeled. Most boxes labeled, some missing a field, or a drawing left unlabeled. Boxes unlabeled or out of order, so I cannot tell which competency a box belongs to.
Accuracy and organization The physiology is correct, and it is organized as sketches, sequences, flow charts or short lists. Information is arranged so the relationships are visible. Mostly correct, with some errors carried into the second pass uncorrected, or organization that is mostly prose with a few sketches. Significant errors, or paragraphs of copied text with no structure. Writing out a lot of words is not the same as organizing the material.
Completeness All fourteen boxes present, both passes visible in both colors, nothing left blank. Boxes missing, or a second pass on only some of them. Large sections missing, or only one pass done.
Following directions and effort Handwritten on paper, two distinct colors, second pass added into the original box, prompt actually answered rather than the topic summarized. Directions partly followed. Typed sections, one color only, or a second pass started on a fresh page. Directions not followed. Digital handwriting, one color, or the box does not answer the prompt that was chosen.

The two things that cost the most points

One color. If both passes are in the same pen, I cannot see what you learned from the videos, and that comparison is the reason this assignment exists. It is the single most common way to lose marks here.

Paragraphs. A box filled edge to edge with sentences copied from the book scores lower than a half full box with a correct labeled sketch. Copying is not the same as working with the material, and it is obvious on the page.
